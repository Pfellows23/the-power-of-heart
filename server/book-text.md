Introduction

When you come to Mauna Kea Hotel, located within Mauna Kea Resort, you feel and recognize that you are in an extraordinary place. And it is—it’s a place that offers tremendous gifts, treasures and connections that last a lifetime.

The experiences you have here, and the people you meet and share time with, will always hold a special place in your heart.

This book is a collection of quotations, poems and reflections of the heart wisdom that found me while I was at Mauna Kea Beach Hotel, at a crossroads in my life.

While these sentiments are personal to my experience here, I know that I am not the only one who has stumbled upon a healing heart’s journey in this magical place.

The hearts arrived as literal heart gifts that washed onto Kauna'oa Beach as I walked along the shore. White pumice, coral-like heart stones, delivered by the sea.

In only a few weeks, 127 heart shaped stones had washed across my path.

Each one that came spurred my heart into a deep reflection that helped propel me further on my path towards and with commitment to the discovery and realization of unconditional, soul-purpose love and self-actualization.

I share quotes from artists and teachers whose names you may recognize, like Buddha, Rumi, Pele, Dave Matthews, and others. My intent is to share the timeless, ageless, common thread that’s woven through all their words and ideas, which is the essence of Love.

Love has been the only thing in life that has truly ever interested me. The many expressions of it, depths of it, and how it shines through the various roles, relationships and phases of our lives.

This book is dedicated to everyone who loves Mauna Kea Beach Hotel & Resort and the special moments you experience here.

With heart, Pamela

A Place of Connection

A friend of mine at who protects and watches over Kauna'oa Bay, told me that when Laurance Rockefeller flew over the big island of Hawaii in search of a site to develop, he
was drawn to this special natural inlet because he saw light and illumination coming from the shore.

He built this resort before it made sense to anyone else. There was no road from the airport along the Kona Coast at that time, and it was not easy to reach the location. But he made his choice from a knowledge and intuition he had within himself, a recognition of the purity and uniqueness of the place that set it apart.

When they developed the land, they paid special attention to the natural lines and energy
of the land, and built in a style that came to be referred to as 'supportive development.

I see it as an extension of the care and connection that Mauna Kea Resort represents, and
is part of its legacy of relationship, care and connection.

It’s no wonder that families, friends, loves, lovers, and individuals have been coming here for decades to marry, celebrate, meet, say goodbye or simply be at Mauna Kea Resort in the presence of true connection, love, care, aloha, and all that is there to discover.

My Meeting of Mauna Kea

I was introduced to Mauna Kea Resort in 2017 on my first trip ever to Hawaii while on an acting retreat led by a dear teacher, mentor and friend, Jim Jarrett. He described it as a place of healing and of heart.

He shared that it should not be thought of as just another nice resort. If you did that, he said, you would miss out on experiencing the essence of the gifts that are offered at this very special place.

He told me that at the Mauna Kea, I could have the opportunity to learn what Aloha is truly about from those who live and work with intention in their hearts to honor, protect and care for this special destination. Anyone who brings heartful awareness to the Mauna Kea, will have the opportunity to be transformed by the experience of being here. It's no wonder I found myself here at this point in my life.

A time when the life I had planned for myself had fallen apart. Or it was just falling into place in a way I had not expected or intended? My nine-year marriage had ended, and the big island was where we came to transition through the divorce process and into a new life. As a single, full-time parent on the verge of turning forty, my life was full of unknowns, concerns, disappointments and unfulfilled intentions and hopes. Though my heart was heavy, in my heart, I had a hunch the Mauna Kea would play a role in my healing and at this crossroad.

I’ve noticed the Mauna Kea attracts those who, on some level, seek and appreciate depth of purpose in life, and connection with others. Those who have an appreciation of and are driven to find freedom, peace and joy for their soul and in their hearts. The Mauna Kea was very much a pivotal place in my heart's journey.

The Perfect Heart

One heart in particular inspired this book.

Well, one heart stone, and a combination of heartfelt moments that were shared here.

This heart is one my daughter Talulah found on one of the many visits my children and I took to Mauna Kea Resort while living on island.

We came to call this one "the perfect heart". There was an encounter I experienced at Mauna Kea as well that rocked my world like the eruption of the Maunakea itself.

In the weeks following the finding of this heart, 127 more white coral heart rocks washed across my path on Mauna Kea beach.

One by one I gathered them. With each one, I sat and took in the depth of love and connection I felt being on island and from these times at the Mauna Kea.

I reflected on life, love and the ebbs and flows that come in and out of each.

Knowing no rock or shell should be taken off island,*each heart served as an invitation of reflection and each was later released back to the ocean.

This was a fitting practice for me to go through as I was experiencing life continuing to ask me to let go.

Let go of the expectations I had for myself and for what I had wanted for my life and love.

Let go of the grief, the sadness, the shame, the heaviness, my ego.

I am grateful that the hearts of the Mauna Kea invited me to keep my heart open and receptive, without attachment, like the beach.

Each heart also connected me into my own heart and I noticed, each time, that what was there was a love that flowed like lava and was wanting to burst out.

*Pele is a Hawaiian goddess who is the Island’s matriarch and protector. She is incredibly protective of her lands and children. Legend has it that Pele considers the lava rocks and sand as her children. Therefore, it is in alignment and in good etiquette to respect her by recognizing that the jewels of nature that are there, are to be left there. If anyone “steals” the rocks or sand, Pele will teach them the proper manners through what can be described as hard lessons or a curse to remind people to respect the Mauna and power of the natural energy and gifts of Hawaii.

On the night of November 19, 2021 there was a lunar ecIipse of the November Supermoon.

This marked the longest partial lunar eclipse of a Supermoon since February 18, 1440, and the longest that will be until February 8, 2669. This was also the eve of my 40th birthday.

I sat on the windy beach, alone and looking up at the moon, I asked for a sign. It was the kind of talk you have with yourself and the greater cosmos in the hope that someone and something greater than yourself is listening. A heart to heart, star prayer kind of talk. I asked, what was next in my life? Where do I go from here? What would love look like in my life now? What is my highest path from here forward? I knew I had so much to give.

I sent out a signal, a star wish S.O.S, to have something put on my path to show me the way. I surrendered to what was beyond what I knew and could see...

Life Can Change in a Moment

As I walked down to the beach on a stone laid path, coffee in hand, I felt a quickening of energy coming from behind me.

A force so potent it was physical.

I was moved to step to the side of the path.

I turned to offer space, with curiosity to see what or who it was I had so powerfully sensed approaching. That's when I saw him, someone I had never met but felt like I had always known and recognized immediately.

I managed to utter a 'good morning' or 'hello' as our eyes met and he walked by. The moment had me fully and completely stopped in my tracks.

It took my breath away.

My heart has not been the same since. It was in that moment I remembered my star wish from the night before and I was reminded of something I often forget, which is: sometimes the prayers we ask for and answers we seek come in very quickly!

I was standing in one of those moments that can often happen at the Mauna Kea...       a meeting of worlds. Awe striking moments.

This was a collision of universes.

’Aumakua

A friend who I had the pleasure of meeting on island is a talented Hawaiian artist who makes hand carved pendants and sculptures. His work very much carries the essence of Hawaiian culture, tradition, and the ocean.

I asked him how he would define what the Hawaiian word 'aumakua means.

He said:

"’Aumakua refers to the messengers from the spirit worlds who are our guardian spirits"...

Each family has a deep connection to a spirit animal or a creature from the key elements. Water land and sky. Meaning, our relatives and ancestors have taken shapes of different forms of life.

Hawaiians believe that we are one with all things and all things are one with us.

We are all connected in mind body and spirit. Prayers and chants are often recited recognizing the higher power of those before us and all things on earth, in the sense that we are still communicating today. Our 'aumakua brings great symbolism and teachings that can guide our lives."

- Ben Muti

The Legend of Naupaka

On a nighttime walk through the Mauna Kea halls, exploring the art amid the open air walkways, I learned about a unique flowering plant that grows near the shore called Naupaka Kahakai. Among the green leaves of the Naupaka Kahakai, small white flowers bloom with what appear to only be half their petals.

Curiously, a similar plant by a different name, Naupaka Kuahiwi, grows on the high mountains with the same half  bloomed white petaled flowers. These plants are said represent the Legend of Naupaka.

On an early morning walk by the shore, Princess Naupaka saw a handsome fisherman. The fisherman, named Kuai, noticed the Princess as he was casting his net into the lagoon. It was love at first sight. Given their different positions in life, they feared their love could never be. Many people were jealous—among them the Goddess Pele herself, who was sister to Princess Naupaka. There was much disapproval and gossip in both their communities. The Gods and Kahunas did not favor the connection. Still, as it happens with great love, they tried to find a way.

Across the various versions of the Legend, I did not find one in which Princess Naupaka and Kuai end up together. Still, in each version, they chose to care for the other, with great sacrifice of their own wants and desires to be together, in order to protect and honor the other. What beauty there is in the power of that kind of love, the unconditional kind. The kind that led them through doubts, adversity, challenges, life and death, trusting something they felt, experienced and knew between them. The indescribable experience of meeting a twin soul, a mirrored love, is often hard to describe, and hard for those who don’t know it to fully understand.

Their connection was one that was felt across a great distance, like the flowers blooming from the seashore to the mountains. But only half blooming in each place, to depict the missing piece that resides in the heart of the other. May we all bloom in the face of our heartaches, transitions, and in times of sorrow or separation. May we all find joy in the beauty of these flowers and a reminder of great loves that have come across our lives and of the loves that never leave us and co-create our story of existing at all.

A note from the Author

I created this book from the deep love in my heart and for who I met and what I experienced at  Mauna Kea Beach Hotel - moments that touched my soul and reminded me that life is serendipitously perfect and divine.

The writing of it began while I lived on island and was and on the brink of a new beginning in my life. It was a time my heart was breaking open from inner transformations in my path of meditation, self realization, my connection to the island itself, while transitioning out of my marriage and building anew.

I credit the beach and the people of the Mauna Kea Resort, that held me through this time and reminded me of the depth of my own heart and spirit and how to be resilient in the face of the unknown. I most deeply feel my connection to the Big Island of Hawaii while at Mauna Kea, often in meditations with the Buddha or in conversation with those I have met here.

I hope in sharing my experience and reflections here, they will touch and contribute to your heart's journey and experiences you have here and carry forward as well.
